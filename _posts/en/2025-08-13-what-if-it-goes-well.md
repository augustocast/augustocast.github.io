Curious, nerdy, or a bit of a control freak. I'm not entirely sure why, but I spend my time reading posts, blogs and papers, with no bigger goal than learning, understanding, or being surprised. But can this be useful for anything beyond collecting trivia and soothing my anxiety?

# Digital native

My earliest memory tied to this compulsive habit is Encarta 2000, one of the very few Microsoft products that were actually worth it[^1]. Same content as a paper encyclopedia, but a far better interface. Instead of ordering articles alphabetically, it grouped them by topic, which was amazing for an 8-year-old. I spent hours learning about countries I'd never heard of, historical figures, and animals.


![Encarta](/assets/2025-06-19-encarta-es.png)

# Scattered
Years later came Wikipedia, the great collective encyclopedia, with an incredible homepage featuring a *Featured article*, *on this day*, and *today's resource*. To this day I still use the free encyclopedia as my first stop for information on countless topics[^2]. It usually has fairly good, reasonably well-organized information to get the big picture on a subject and start pulling the thread.
That can be a risk[^3], since its hyperlinks can exponentially grow the number of open tabs, with Wikipedia entries linking to more Wikipedia entries inside them.

Let's look at an example:


![Argentina](/assets/2025-06-19-wiki-arg-es.png)


In the first two paragraphs of the entry for [Argentina](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina) there are 13 links to other Wikipedia pages. If we click into [democratic](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracia), we can quickly drift toward forms of [social organization](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizaci%C3%B3n_social), [platonism](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonismo), [power](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poder_social_y_pol%C3%ADtico), or [legitimacy](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimidad_(pol%C3%ADtica)). I didn't even try those links, because I'd get so distracted it would become impossible to finish this post.

![Democracy](/assets/2025-06-19-wiki-democracia-es.png)


# Total despair
I keep reading [posts](https://x.com/AlanDaitch/status/1935106507374272671), [blogs](https://www.cuatroveintiuno.com/soberania-cognitiva-introduccion-autonomia-psiquica/) and [papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872). I get hit with the anxiety of losing my cognitive sovereignty and my ability to memorize things. To deal with that, I write. Writing helps me process, think, prioritize and organize. To make sure my notes are actually good, I sometimes think about my future self. It's time to do something about this whole mess.

I started googling and reading posts about methods to systematize my otherwise useless readings. After several unsuccessful attempts at taking notes that would actually be useful later, I settled on five necessary conditions for the method to work:
1. **Portable across devices**: I want to be able to read and write as fast as possible, without having to go find my computer or wait until I'm home to write something down.
2. **Easy to maintain**: I need to keep a low cognitive load. I want to focus on what I write, not on how or where I store it, or how I'll find it later.
3. **Able to import web notes**: essential so I don't end up with a hundred open tabs, or have to re-read everything from scratch.
4. **Good search mechanism**: it's essential to quickly find what I'm looking for. Otherwise, it doesn't scale.
5. **Simple to export**: some format that lets me share my notes without much friction.

# The solution
I found Thiago Forte's [PARA Method](https://www.buildingasecondbrain.com/para). It suggests splitting our notes into 4 sections, from most to least frequently used:
- Projects: with a beginning and an end.
- Areas: things I care about and need to maintain.
- Resources: things I care about but don't plan to maintain.
- Archives: things I no longer care about, but might again someday.

After some back and forth on whether a note belonged in Areas or Resources — settled by [this Reddit post](https://www.reddit.com/r/PKMS/comments/wam0k3/im_still_hooked_up_on_areas_vs_resources_in_para/) — I was ready to build my *Second Brain*.

Forte's argument is a bit reductive: if you take good notes and can later retrieve them, you can effectively "remember" everything you've read and build a second brain. It sounds like a spin-off of [Marie Kondo](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/37864838-la-magia-del-orden)'s obsession with order, but honestly, I don't mind. A [bad rule](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/030439328390051X) tends to beat good discretion. I now have a simple method for taking notes that's easy to maintain and easy to search.

# The tool
A good tool to implement the PARA Method and we're all set. I chose [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/) because:
1. It's portable across devices.
2. Its interface is simple, with a **dark theme**.
3. It has tags and links between notes.
4. Its Chrome plugin lets you import web pages.
5. It's written in Markdown, which is simple to export.

I won't get into the classic [Obsidian vs. Notion](https://www.reddit.com/r/productivity/comments/zvojcq/notion_vs_obsidian/) debate in this post, because trying to cover too much ends up covering nothing well. I didn't know how to use either one, and I wanted to focus on taking notes, not on learning a new tool, so I went with the simpler one. So far, no regrets.

# Prototyping Mindset

The PARA Method is great, but it's not enough on its own to change my habits. I want to build a *Second Brain* that lets me remember everything I've read that was worth it. Or at least part of it. For that, I need to keep writing, and figuring out whether what I write is a Project, an Area, or a Resource. I'm going to exercise my [prototyping mindset](https://benholliday.medium.com/the-prototyping-mindset-320de6ec79c8): write with production in mind. That means doing it fast, with the goal of learning something and iterating. If it goes well, I'll be writing about it. If it goes badly, I'll have learned a lot of things.

My commitment: I write my own posts, ChatGPT doesn't write them for me. Otherwise, they're worthless to me.
